# Anchors And The Selection Authority

A position that must survive commits is an anchor: a CRDT relative position, minted when the position becomes interesting and resolved when it is used. `AN` is the anchor contract; `AS` is how the selection authority holds, repairs, and resolves its record. Contracts live in `@input/pen-types` (`types/anchors.ts` plus the two `CRDTAdapter` methods), the encoding in `@input/pen-yjs`, and the runtime in `@input/pen-core` (`editor.anchors`, the resolution cache, `anchorRepair.ts`, and `SelectionAuthority`). No library code maps a raw `{ blockId, offset }` across commits and none re-locates content by fingerprint (I13).

## AN — Anchor Contract

- AN1. Resolution is total by adapter policy, not by underlying CRDT behavior: `resolve` returns a live `AnchorTarget` with its offset clamped into the block's logical length, or `null`, and never throws or returns an out-of-range offset. `null` means the encoded item is not in this store or its type is gone from the blocks map, including a deleted block — it does not mean the referenced characters were deleted, which collapse to a live index instead. Consumers distinguish a store miss (retry on the next commit) from a blocks-map miss by checking whether the block exists.
- AN2. Insertion-side stability: content inserted exactly at an anchor leaves an `assoc: -1` anchor before the insertion and moves an `assoc: 1` anchor after it. The exception is an `assoc: 1` anchor whose character was deleted. It stays bound to that character's tombstone and resolves to the collapsed index (AN1), and Yjs integrates text inserted at that index after the adjacent tombstones, so the anchor stays before the insertion as an `assoc: -1` anchor would. This holds for local applies, remote updates, and stream flushes alike, and repair does not correct it, because re-minting on an ordinary commit is what AN4 forbids.
- AN3. Convergence holds with one exception: two peers resolving the same anchor against converged documents obtain the same target for ordinary edit interleavings. Undo of a deletion the anchor's item participated in is the measured exception, and the per-provenance resolver policy (AN13) makes shared paths converge by construction.
- AN4. Survival: an anchor survives any interleaving of in-block local applies, remote updates, undo/redo, and stream flushes without being re-minted. The repair step (AN14) is the single sanctioned re-mint site; code that re-mints per ordinary commit has reimplemented mapping and violates I13.
- AN5. Deletion collapse: an `AnchorRange` whose interior content was entirely deleted resolves with `collapsed: true`, both endpoints at the deletion point. This is the range-destroyed signal. Content inserted between the endpoints does not collapse a range.
- AN6. Transport safety: `serialize` and `deserialize` round-trip across sessions and peers, and `deserialize` never throws. A deserialized anchor is untrusted input — a hostile, oversize, truncated, malformed, or unknown-version payload returns `null` with `diagnostic { code: "anchor-decode" }`, and a payload that survives decode still degrades to `null` at resolve per AN1.
- AN7. GC envelope: under the workspace `gc: false` posture, position identity persists indefinitely, including through delete-then-undo, because tombstoned items keep their identity. If a host enables compaction, anchors into collected regions resolve to `null`; that degradation is stated, not silent.
- AN8. Resolution cache: core memoizes `resolve` per anchor identity and `commitId`, so repeated resolution of the same anchor within one commit costs one adapter call. The cache is invalidated by the next commit, holds weak references to anchors, and is mandatory — consumers do not build their own.
- AN9. Anchor budget: more than 4,096 live anchors per editor emits `diagnostic { code: "anchor-budget" }` once per session and identifies the minting site in its payload. It is a smell detector for `O(content)` anchor populations, not a hard cap; anchors above the budget still function. `liveCount` counts mints, deserializes, and re-mints without decrementing, so the diagnostic fires on churn as well as on a large simultaneous set.
- AN10. Cell addressing: anchors address any text the adapter can reach — block text with `cell` absent, table cell text with `cell` present, matching the cell addressing `splice-text` uses. The selection authority uses block-level targets for text selections and cell targets for the `text` range of a cell being edited; other cell-text anchors exist for extension consumers.
- AN11. Wire format: `serialize` produces JSON of the form `{ "v": 1, "b": blockId, "a": -1 | 1, "c": [row, col]?, "p": base64(position) }`. The encoded position is capped at 256 bytes as a hostile-input bound rather than a working budget, and receivers reject an unknown `v` to `null` instead of guessing, so `v` is bumped only by amending this rule. Provenance is not carried on the wire: anything that crossed a wire or a session is wire-form by construction (AN13).
- AN12. Anchors are values: `Anchor` is deep-frozen plain data. Nothing about an anchor changes after minting, and resolution state lives in the cache rather than on the anchor.
- AN13. Resolver policy follows provenance, never a caller argument. Anchors minted locally and never shared resolve with `followUndoneDeletions: true`, which is correct for their owner through that owner's undo. Anchors that arrived from a wire or an earlier session resolve with `followUndoneDeletions: false`, which is convergent across all receivers at the documented cost that a position restored through another peer's undo sits at the deletion boundary rather than on the restored character. `create` always stamps local provenance and `deserialize` always stamps wire provenance, so callers cannot express a preference and cannot get this wrong.
- AN14. Anchors do not follow split and merge; the repair step does. Because the executors move content by copy, every anchor-holding consumer runs `deriveContentMoves` and `repairAnchor` before resolving any commit that reports a content move — local moves from the summary's `block-split` / `blocks-merged` recipe, remote ones from same-length delete/insert pairing across blocks — and a re-mint preserves the anchor's provenance. The gate is a reported move, never the shape of `structural`, so a remote peer's split still repairs a local position; repair reads the pre-commit target rather than a post-commit resolve, and is a no-op on every ordinary text commit. The pre-commit target is the anchor's most recent mint, re-mint, or resolve from before the current commit. A resolve made after the commit landed does not replace it, by this consumer or by any other holder of the same anchor value, and an observation of `null` counts, so repair never revives a dead anchor from an older live position. A consumer that does not resolve its anchors on every commit therefore repairs from wherever they last resolved. An anchor on an edge of a moved range stays with the text on its side of that edge, unless the commit removed the source block and left nothing on that side, in which case it moves with the range; this is what carries an `assoc: -1` anchor at the start of a merged-away block into the target. An anchor shared with the selection authority is repaired once, by the authority; a consumer sharing it takes the authority's repair through `editor.internals.selectionAnchorRepair(anchor, commitId)` instead of repairing it a second time.

Minting can fail. `create` and `range` return `null` with `diagnostic { code: "anchor-target-missing" }` when the adapter cannot mint — a missing block or cell, a block with no text such as a nested container, section, or divider, or an already-deleted type — and never throw.

## AS — Selection Authority Internals

- AS1. On every accepted text-selection write, the authority mints anchors for anchor and focus at write time: non-collapsed selections use `anchorAssoc: -1` with `focusAssoc: 1`, and collapsed selections use `assoc: 1` on both. `affinity` and `goalX` stay on the record as display state, not position identity. An accepted cell selection carrying `text` mints cell-text anchors for its anchor and focus the same way, and AS2 repairs and resolves them with the text endpoints. A text endpoint on a block with no text, such as a divider or table, mints nothing, so the authority holds no anchors for that selection. `editor.internals.selectionAnchors()` exposes the held pair to extensions that need the same positions (undo's drift), so they share the authority's anchors instead of minting a second pair.
- AS2. The commit phase that updates selection is repair-then-resolve: the retained anchors pass through the repair step first, then resolve. If the resolved targets differ from the record's current points, the authority writes the mapped state with `origin: "mapped"` and the commit's `commitId`; if they are identical, the record is untouched and its version is stable across the commit. The authority's own anchors are local provenance.
- AS3. Non-text selections are driven by the summary's structural data rather than by anchors: block selections drop removed IDs through the fallback chain, cell selections clamp into the post-commit grid, and a table change resets. The nearest-sibling retarget when a block is removed — including a text caret whose block vanished — is this mapping, because anchors resolve that case to `null`. Only in-block text-point identity lives on anchors.
- AS4. The selection authority imports no cross-commit position-mapping module. This is a grep gate, not a convention.
- AS5. A full document swap releases every anchor the authority holds. Anchors minted against the old document are invalid against the new one by construction, since the CRDT identities differ, and resolving a stale anchor is `null` per AN1.
